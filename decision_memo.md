# Experiment Readout Memo — Checkout Flow Redesign

**To:** Checkout product team · **From:** Data Science · **Date:** 2026-10-24
**Decision: ✅ SHIP to 100%**, with a 1% holdback for two weeks (see Next steps).

> Data note: this experiment was run on a **simulated dataset with known ground
> truth** (control 12.0%, true lift +1.5pp) generated by the seeded simulator in
> `src/generate_data.py`. All figures below are the analysis output on that
> data, produced by the notebook in `notebooks/`.

## Hypothesis

Replacing the multi-step checkout with a streamlined single-page flow reduces
friction enough to raise checkout completion by at least +1.0pp absolute
(the smallest lift worth the migration cost) without degrading guardrails.

## Design

- **Randomization:** user-level, 50/50, 100,000 users (50,000/arm), fixed-horizon.
- **Primary metric:** checkout conversion. **α = 0.05**, two-sided.
- **Power:** at a 12% baseline, 50,000/arm detects **+0.58pp** at 80% power
  (the +1.5pp design effect needs only ~7,800/arm — the test was deliberately
  overpowered here; flagged as a design lesson below).
- **Pre-declared segments:** user type (new/returning), device (mobile/desktop),
  judged at Bonferroni α = 0.0125.
- **Variance reduction:** CUPED on prior-30-day sessions (pre-experiment).

## Results

- **Data quality:** no SRM (χ² = 0.000, p = 1.000; exact 50/50 split).
  Pre-experiment covariate balanced (prior sessions 6.84 vs. 6.78);
  segment mix balanced (returning 60.4% vs. 59.8%; mobile 62.0% vs. 61.9%).
- **Primary:** control 11.90% → treatment 13.43%. **Effect +1.53pp,
  95% CI [+1.12, +1.94]pp**, z = 7.27, **p = 3.5e-13** (+12.9% relative).
  Chi-square cross-check gives the identical p-value. The CI's lower bound
  alone exceeds the +1.0pp materiality bar in the hypothesis.
- **Bayesian:** P(B > A) = 1.0000 (no losing draws in 200,000 posterior
  samples); posterior mean +1.53pp; **expected loss of shipping B = 0.000000**.
- **CUPED:** adjusted effect +1.63pp with SE reduced 3.0% (outcome variance
  −5.8%, θ = 0.0170, covariate ρ = 0.241) — corroborates the primary result.
- **Segments:** all four positive and significant after Bonferroni —
  new +1.54pp, returning +1.67pp, mobile +1.58pp, desktop +1.44pp. No harmed
  subgroup; intervals consistent with the overall effect. (New users' +43%
  *relative* lift is a low-baseline artifact — absolute lifts are uniform.)
- **Test audit (possible only because ground truth is known):** replaying
  1,000 null experiments gives an empirical false-positive rate of **0.054**
  vs. nominal 0.05 — the test is calibrated. At the true +1.5pp effect,
  empirical power is **1.000** (1,000/1,000).

## Guardrails

Refund rate, support-contact rate, and checkout latency are **not present in
this dataset**, so no guardrail verdict is possible here. In a production
readout this ship call would be conditional on all three being flat; treat
that as an open item, not a pass.

## Decision

**Ship.** The effect is decisive on every lens we have — frequentist,
Bayesian, CUPED-adjusted, and segment-level — the downside risk measured by
expected loss is nil, and no subgroup is harmed.

## Next steps

1. Ramp to 100% behind a **1% holdback for two weeks** to validate the
   long-term effect against the +1.12pp CI lower bound (novelty check).
2. Monitor conversion weekly for the first month post-launch.
3. Wire refund/support/latency guardrails into the experiment platform
   **before** the next checkout test — a conversion-only readout is incomplete.
4. Make CUPED on `prior_sessions` the default adjustment for checkout
   experiments (≈6% variance reduction ≈ 6% less traffic for the same power).
5. Right-size future designs: pre-register ~8,000 users/arm for +1.5pp-class
   effects on this surface; reserve 50k/arm for decisions that genuinely
   hinge on ~0.6pp.
